A day. Two weeks. Or a seat at the table.

  • BKick-start — launch an internal innovation sprint

Human-centered design research on one problem space — real customers, real interviews — accelerated by AI and guardrailed by people.

02 — How we think

Two frameworks. One tells you where to look. The other tells you what to pick.

Where to look · Three questions for any leadership team

Hear. See. Feel.

Most companies don’t have a strategy problem. They have a senses problem.

Every company runs on three senses. When one goes quiet, decisions get made on stale or partial information — and it shows up as a strategy problem long before anyone names the real cause.

Ask the three questions. The one that’s hardest to answer tells you where to start.

01

Hear

What do you hear from your clients?

When it’s working

Client feedback arrives unprompted, reaches the people who can act on it, and visibly changes something.

If you aren’t hearing

You have a client communication and feedback problem.

02

See

What do you see your competitors doing?

When it’s working

You know what competitors shipped, priced and hired for last quarter — and someone owns what it means for you.

If you aren’t seeing

You have a business intelligence and monitoring problem.

03

Feel

What do you feel you need to do to move the company forward?

When it’s working

Leadership shares a clear conviction about the next move — and protects the time on the calendar to keep forming it.

If you aren’t feeling

You aren’t giving yourselves the time and space to be strategic about the business.

How we use it

Hear · See · Feel is the first hour of every Thumb Studio engagement. Which sense is failing decides what we build.

Hear → listening loops  ·  See → intelligence systems  ·  Feel → strategic cadence
Then

One tells you where to look. The other tells you what to pick.

What to pick · Three lenses for choosing a bet

Capability. Urgency. Durability.

Can we. Must they. Should we.

Hear · See · Feel tells you where your company is blind. This one tells you which bet to take once you can see. Every opportunity space gets three scores from 1 to 5.

A weak answer on any one lens is the answer — however strong the other two.

01

Capability

Can we do this — or is it an adjacency worth building?

The feasibility question, asked like an operator. Low capability isn’t a no if it’s a capability you want to own.

  1. 5We have it in-house today.
  2. 3Adjacent — buildable or buyable within a year, and worth having.
  3. 1Someone else’s business.
02

Urgency

Is this a painkiller or a vitamin — and for how many?

The desirability question, with teeth. Depth of pain first, then breadth. A painkiller for six people is a feature, not a bet.

  1. 5Painkiller. They already pay for, or hack around, the problem.
  2. 3A known irritation. Nobody has budget for it yet.
  3. 1Vitamin. Nice to have; nobody is asking.
03

Durability

Should we — does it fit our business now, and still matter in five years?

The viability question, pointed forward. Fit with where the business is going, and whether it grows with you or burns out.

  1. 5Grows with us. Matters more in five years.
  2. 3Makes sense now. Unclear whether it compounds or plateaus.
  3. 1A one-off, a trend, or a detour.
How to score

Scores don’t add. A 5 · 5 · 1 is a dead idea, not an 11 out of 15. Rank by the lowest score, and argue about the floor.

Used to rank the opportunity spaces at the end of every Sprint, and to converge in every Workshop. Adapted from the desirability · feasibility · viability lens popularized by IDEO.
